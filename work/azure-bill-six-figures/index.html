Work

An Azure bill that had quietly grown by six figures

Cloud cost reduction for a global accountancy training organisation.

£150,000a year removed from the Azure bill
Hundredsunused resources deleted, all still being paid for

The problem

Spend had grown faster than anyone could explain. Nobody owned the bill end to end, several third parties had built into the estate over the years, and the finance question ("why is this going up?") had no technical answer.

What we did

Audited the estate end to end: every resource, what it cost, what it was attached to and whether anything still depended on it. That produced a ranked list of candidates with numbers against them. We then checked the list with the internal team and the third parties who had built into the estate over the years, because an audit can tell you a resource is idle but not whether someone is about to need it. Hundreds of orphaned resources were removed, virtual machines moved onto reserved instances where the usage pattern justified it, and savings plans applied to app services.

Where it landed

A recurring saving of £150,000 a year, and a cost picture the team could keep current themselves.

What to take from this

  • Most cloud overspend is not inefficiency, it is abandoned work nobody has permission to delete.
  • The audit finds the waste. The people who own it tell you what is safe to delete, and that second step is what stops a cost exercise causing an outage.
  • Reserved instances only pay off where the usage pattern is genuinely stable. Buy them for the wrong workload and you have just prepaid for waste.
  • A saving that nobody can maintain comes back within a year. Leave the team the workbook.
