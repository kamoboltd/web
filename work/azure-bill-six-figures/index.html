Work

An Azure bill that had quietly grown by six figures

Cloud cost reduction for a global accountancy training organisation.

£150,000a year removed from the Azure bill
Hundredsof orphaned resources deleted

The problem

Spend had grown faster than anyone could explain. Nobody owned the bill end to end, several third parties had built into the estate over the years, and the finance question ("why is this going up?") had no technical answer.

What we did

Started with people rather than dashboards: interviewed the third parties and the internal team to find out what was actually in use and what nobody would miss. Then moved virtual machines onto reserved instances where the usage pattern justified it, applied savings plans to app services, and removed hundreds of orphaned resources that were still being paid for.

Where it landed

A recurring saving of £150,000 a year, and a cost picture the team could keep current themselves.

What to take from this

  • Most cloud overspend is not inefficiency, it is abandoned work nobody has permission to delete.
  • Interviews find more savings than tooling does, because the tooling cannot tell you what is no longer needed.
  • Reserved instances only pay off where the usage pattern is genuinely stable. Buy them for the wrong workload and you have just prepaid for waste.
  • A saving that nobody can maintain comes back within a year. Leave the team the workbook.
